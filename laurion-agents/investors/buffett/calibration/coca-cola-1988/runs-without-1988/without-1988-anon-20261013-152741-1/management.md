# Stage 3: Management and capital allocation (Framework §4)

§4 has three rules, which I apply in the order the framework lists them:

1. Honest, able management already in place, with candid reporting (`brk-letter-1979`, `brk-letter-1983#2`).
2. Buybacks only when the price is well below intrinsic value (`brk-letter-1984#4`).
3. Each $1 of retained earnings must create at least $1 of market value (`brk-letter-1983`).

I take the third rule first because it is the summary test of everything else. The other two then explain the result.

---

## 4c. Each $1 retained must create at least $1 of market value

The 1987 letter says the current team was assembled in 1981, so I measure from the end of 1980.

**Market value created.**
- End-1980 price was $11.13 (split-adjusted) on about 372M average shares, or about **$4.14B**.
- At the last close of $46.56 on 372.4M shares (end-1987 count), it is about **$17.34B**. 1988 buybacks are not in the packet.
- Increase: about **$13.2B**. This excludes the entertainment-affiliate shares paid out in 1988, which were carried at $335M.

**Earnings retained, 1981–87.** I take net income less cash dividends, and treat the 1987 in-kind dividend as a distribution.

| Year | Earnings | Dividends | Retained (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1981 | 432 (cont. ops) | 59.5% payout | ≈175 |
| 1982 | 494 | 62.8% | ≈184 |
| 1983 | 553 | 65.3% | ≈192 |
| 1984 | 629 | 364 | ≈265 |
| 1985 | 722 | 389 | ≈333 |
| 1986 | 934 | 403 | ≈532 |
| 1987 | 916 | 422 cash + 335 in-kind | ≈159 |
| **Total** | | | **≈$1.84B** |

1988 retained earnings are not in the packet. Even if 1988 retained twice the 1987 figure, the cumulative total stays under $3B.

**Result.** Market value rose about $13B on under $3B retained, more than $4 of value for each $1. During the same period the company also spent about $1.43B on buybacks (1984–87) and paid out the entertainment spin-off. The test is passed by a wide margin.

**Caveat on the size of the margin.** Part of the gain is a re-rating, not a business result. The P/E went from about 10.5x (1980: $11.13 on about $1.06 EPS) to about 19x today. To check the business on its own, I compare the earnings increase with the capital added:
- Income from continuing operations rose from $394M (1980) to $916M (1987), an increase of about **$522M**.
- Capital added was about $1.84B retained, plus roughly $0.6–0.9B of added net debt. Net debt went from about zero in 1980 to the company's 15.3% net debt-to-capital ratio in 1987. Total: about **$2.5–2.7B**.
- That is an after-tax return of roughly 20% on new capital, before adding back interest.

Part of that 20% is currency translation, since key currencies rose 16% in 1987 and 27% in 1986. Even so, the retained money earned at about the same rate as the existing business, which is the point of the test.

## 4b. Buybacks only well below intrinsic value

The record, computed from treasury purchases in the equity and funds statements:

| Year | Shares bought | Cost | Avg. price | Contemporary EPS | P/E paid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1984 | 18.4M | $335.7M | ≈$18.24 | $1.57 | ≈11.6x |
| 1985 | 15.0M | $379.9M | ≈$25.33 | $1.72 | ≈14.7x |
| 1986 | 2.9M | $110.5M | ≈$38.37 | ≈$2.07 ex-items | ≈18.5x |
| 1987 | 14.2M | $605.1M | ≈$42.61 | $2.43 | ≈17.5x |
| Program | 40M authorized through 1990 (6.6M done by YE87 at ≈$42.12) | | | | |

**What the record shows.**
- *Demonstrated, not just announced.* `brk-letter-1984#4` says the key word is "demonstrated." The company retired about 50M shares over four years, more than 12% of the share count, and average shares fell from 408M (1983) to 377M (1987). This is net of option exercises of about 1.1–1.3M shares a year.
- *Early purchases were clearly cheap.* At $18–25 a share and 11–15x earnings, for a business earning about 20%+ unlevered on equity with owner earnings near reported earnings (Stage 2), the 1984–85 purchases were almost certainly well below intrinsic value. At today's $46.56 they look like the "$2 of present value for $1" the passage describes.
- *Later purchases are less clear.* The 1986–87 purchases were made at 17–19x. Whether they were "well below" value depends on the Stage 5 estimate, so I leave that open here.
- *The stated reason is about cash, not price.* The MD&A says repurchases began "after management determined that the Company's available cash and debt capacity exceeded the funds required." That is an excess-cash rationale, not an explicit price-versus-value test. A management that buys back stock because it has spare cash, whatever the price, could keep buying at prices above value. The packet does not show whether this management would stop.
- *Funded partly with debt.* Short-term borrowings rose by about $1.0B in 1987, mostly for buybacks and bottler stakes. That is acceptable at a 15% net debt ratio, but it ties into the leverage point below.

**On this rule:** passes. The history is strongly owner-oriented, and the early purchases were exactly what the rule describes. The open question is whether management would stop buying if the price went above value.

## 4a. Honest, able management in place; candid reporting

### Ability, shown through reallocation of capital since 1981

**Pruning outside the core:**
- Sold Ronco pasta (1984).
- Sold Presto and Winkler plastics (1985).
- Shut down or sold bottling interests in South Africa, Guatemala, and partly Australia and Japan.
- Sold Embassy Pictures and Home Entertainment (1986), recovering "virtually all" of the 1985 Embassy investment.

**Concentrating on the concentrate business.** Soft drinks now produce more than 95% of operating income. The 1987 report says management will prefer "investments within our existing lines of business" and will judge them against an explicit after-tax cost of capital of about 12%. A stated hurdle rate is rare and worth noting.

**Separating the capital-heavy businesses:**
- The bottling affiliate was IPO'd in 1986, keeping 49%.
- The Canadian bottler followed in 1987, also keeping 49%.
- Entertainment was combined with the 30%-owned studio and partly spun off to shareholders (1987).

The economic logic holds. Bottling and film need a lot of capital and earn low returns (6% and about 5% on carrying value in 1987, Stage 2), while concentrate does not. Leaving them in separately financed vehicles keeps the parent's capital in the high-return business.

**Retention policy.** Management plans to cut the payout toward 40% "to reinvest more substantially in opportunities offering returns in excess of the cost of capital." Given the 4c result, retaining more is justified, provided the money goes into the core business.

### Where the capital-allocation record is weaker

**The film diversification, 1982–87.**
- Assets in the segment reached $2.17B by 1986, earning about $235M operating income (about 11% pretax).
- The 1985 Embassy purchase was partly paid with about 7.1M shares, which at today's price are worth about $331M. By the standard in `brk-letter-1982#17` (issue shares only when owners receive as much value as they give), using the company's own high-return stock for a low-return business was a poor trade.
- The exit roughly recovered carrying value: market values of the public investees exceeded book by only about $44M at end-1987. So the outcome was not destructive, but it was a long detour away from the core business.

**Foods.** The foods business took a $36M restructuring charge in 1987, had a change in senior management, and earned 11–16% on its assets. It is a mediocre unit that has not yet been fixed or sold.

**Leverage stance.** The 1986 letter has a section headed "Using financial leverage." The target ceiling was raised to 35% net debt to capital explicitly "to reduce the weighted average cost of capital." `fortune-1977-inflation` warns about companies that add debt to prop up returns. This company does not need the borrowing, since net debt is 15.3% and operations generate large cash flows. But the stated willingness to borrow for buybacks and bottler stakes is a habit to watch, and it is part of why recent ROE overstates the underlying return (Stage 2).

**Emphasis on deal gains.** The 1986 IPO gain ($375M) and the 1987 Canadian gain ($40M) arrive alongside restructuring charges ($180M, $36M) in the same years. Every item is disclosed separately, which is fine. But the reports are noticeably absorbed in "innovative business structures," and the gains come from financial transactions, not from selling more drinks.

### Candor in reporting (`brk-letter-1979`, `brk-letter-1983#2`)

**Signs of candor:**
- Unusual items are separated out on both sides, gains as well as charges, and EPS is restated excluding them, both up and down.
- The reports disclose the concentrate gross margin (about 63% in the U.S.), cooperative advertising paid to bottlers ($81M and $153M), the 12% cost-of-capital estimate, currency rate changes by country, and the statement that current-cost earnings "would be less than reported."
- Disappointments are admitted. The 1986 report says "we were not satisfied with the performance of the film studio's feature films." The 1987 report says foods "is obviously in transition."

**Signs of public-relations writing:**
- The 1987 highlights lead with operating income up 47.6%, a figure inflated by 1986's charges. The comparable figure, 23%, appears only in the text.
- The report claims "a $50 billion in sales added an estimated $250 billion to the world economy" using a "commonly accepted multiplier."
- Heavy use of "increasing shareholder value." `brk-letter-1984#4` warns that this kind of phrase means little unless backed by action. Here it is backed by the buybacks, which partly redeems it.
- The 1985 reformulation is described as a "revitalization" of sugar colas, not as a mistake that was reversed.

**On candor:** acceptable but not exemplary. The numbers are complete and the unusual items are honestly separated. The narrative reads like a promotional document of the kind `brk-letter-1979` says owners should not receive.

### Compensation (packet only)

- Equity-linked programs: a restricted stock plan (3M shares authorized, 946,500 still available), the 1983 and 1987 option plans (5.6M options outstanding, about 1.5% of shares), and stock appreciation rights.
- Performance-unit cash awards for officers are based on the stock price above $20.63 across 555,000 shares.
- **Assessment.** Dilution is modest, and buybacks more than offset it. However, the performance units reward the share price, not return on capital or per-share business value. Under §3's primary test (return on equity), that is the wrong yardstick, since a price-based award can be met partly through leverage or re-rating.
- Salaries, bonuses, and the formulas behind them are not in the packet.

## What the investor would have relied on that the packet lacks

1. **Meeting the CEO and president.** How they think about price versus value in buybacks, how they view leverage, whether foods and the remaining film stake are seen as permanent, and their temperament as long-term owners. `brk-letter-1987#18` makes clear that the investor's permanent holdings depended on personally liking and admiring the managers. Nothing in the packet can substitute for that.
2. **The proxy statement.** Officers' and directors' shareholdings ("we eat our own cooking," `brk-letter-1983#0`), salary and bonus, and the bonus formula.
3. **1988 execution.** How many of the 40M authorized shares were bought in 1988 and at what prices, especially after the late-1987 market drop when the stock traded around $36–38.
4. **Reputation inside the system.** How the bottlers, competitors, and former employees regard management's integrity and decisiveness.
5. **Board dynamics.** How independent the board is, and whether it would welcome a large passive shareholder.
6. **Succession plans** and the depth of management below the top two officers.

---

## Stage 3 verdict

Management passes §4, with reservations.

- **4c (retained $1 test):** passed by a wide margin. Even after allowing for the re-rating, retained capital has earned about 20% after tax.
- **4b (buybacks):** passed. The company has repurchased large amounts of stock, and the early purchases were clearly below value. Whether the 1987–88 purchases were still "well below" value is left for Stage 5.
- **4a (management and candor):** since 1981 the team has turned a diversifying company back toward its best business, using a stated cost-of-capital discipline. The reservations are four: the film detour and the stock issued for it, a taste for leverage and deal structures, promotional reporting, and price-based incentive pay.

The record shows owner-oriented management. It does not yet earn the unqualified confidence the investor placed in managers he knew personally, and only meeting them would settle that.
