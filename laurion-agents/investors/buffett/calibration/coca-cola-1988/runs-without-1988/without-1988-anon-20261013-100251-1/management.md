# Stage 3: Management and capital allocation (framework Section 4)

Section 4 sets three tests, applied here in order: (1) honest, able management already in place, with candid reporting; (2) buybacks only well below intrinsic value; (3) each $1 retained must create at least $1 of market value. Acquisitions, debt and compensation are reviewed where they bear on these tests.

---

## 4.1 Honest, able management in place; candid reporting

**Ability: the operating record since the 1981 team took over**

| Measure | 1981 | 1987 |
|---|---|---|
| ROE | 19.9% | 27.2% |
| EPS, continuing operations | $1.17 | $2.43 |
| Debt / capital | 9.1% | 15.3% net (45.6% gross) |
| Share of operating income from soft drinks | — | over 95% |

- Low-return businesses were sold: plastics (Presto, Winkler), pasta (Ronco), and Embassy Pictures and Home Entertainment.
- The company was refocused on concentrate. The 1986 report described "four business sectors." The 1987 report opens with "first and foremost a marketer of soft drinks."
- Operating improvements are concrete: concentrate replaced syrup to cut freight, syrup plants were consolidated, German bottlers went from 102 to 68, and fountain accounts grew by 14,000.
- The same managers who built this record are still in charge. "We can't supply it" (`brk-letter-1982`) is not an issue.

**Candor.** The standard is: "We don't expect a public relations document… and we don't feel you should receive such a document" (`brk-letter-1979`). The reports are mixed.

*Disclosed honestly:*
- The size of one-time items. The 1987 letter itself strips out "35 cents per share of unusual items in 1986."
- U.S. concentrate gross margin of about 63%, and cooperative advertising as a percentage of bottler revenue.
- That current-cost income "would be less than reported," and that purchasing-power gains on debt "do not represent an increase in funds available for distribution."
- Problems: "we were not satisfied with the performance of the film studio"; foods is "obviously in transition."
- Gross debt to capital of 45.6% appears in the selected data alongside management's preferred net figure.

*Promotional:*
- The 1986 headline of EPS up 41%, "best since 1936," included a $375M non-cash gain from CCE's share sale. That gain added 68¢ of the $2.42.
- The 1987 report repeats "increasing shareholder value" more than a dozen times. The 1984 letter warns about managers who mouth "some public relations-inspired phrase such as 'maximizing shareholder wealth' (this season's favorite)" (`brk-letter-1984#4`). The test that letter sets is whether actions match words, and that is taken up in 4.2.
- The claim that the system "added an estimated $250 billion to the world economy" using a "multiplier factor of five" is puffery.
- Language such as "innovative business structures" and "aggressive, efficient use of the financial resources" reads as financial engineering. Combined with yearly restatements (1986 revenue went from $8.67B to $6.98B as units were moved to the equity method), it makes year-to-year comparison harder.

*Off-balance-sheet items to note:*
- Guarantees of $394M.
- A further $325M of CPE receivables guaranteed with recourse.
- Entertainment receivables sold with recourse.

All are disclosed, but they sit outside the headline debt figures.

**Verdict on 4.1:** management is able, with strong evidence. The reports are not dishonest and the material facts are there, but they are more promotional than the framework would like.

## 4.2 Buybacks only well below intrinsic value

The framework's reasoning: "major repurchases at prices well below per-share intrinsic business value immediately increase, in a highly significant way, that value." Buybacks also show that management "is given to actions that enhance the wealth of shareholders," and "the key word is 'demonstrated'" (`brk-letter-1984#4`).

**Repurchase record (cost from the shareholders' equity statements)**

| Year | Shares | Cost | Avg price | P/E on that year's EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1984 | 18.4M | $335.7M | $18.24 | 11.6x |
| 1985 | 15.0M | $379.9M | $25.33 | 14.7x |
| 1986 | 2.9M | $110.5M | $38.37 | 15.9x reported / ~18.5x adjusted |
| 1987 | 14.1M | $605.1M | $42.79 | 17.6x |
| **1984–87** | **~50.4M** | **~$1,431M** | **~$28.40** | |

- About 12% of the 1983 share base was bought in four years. Average shares fell from 408M (1983) to 372M at the end of 1987, net of option exercises and shares issued for acquisitions.
- A further 40M-share program runs to the end of 1990. Only 6.6M had been bought by the end of 1987, so most of it falls in 1988–90, at prices this packet does not show.
- Every repurchase was below today's $46.56. The 1984–85 purchases were at about 40–55% of today's price.

**Were they "well below" intrinsic value?** Stage 2 found a business earning about 25% unlevered on capital, with owner earnings near $2.30 a share and growing. On that basis:
- Buying at 11–15x earnings in 1984–85 was buying well below value.
- The 1986–87 purchases at 16–18x are below value only if the growth runway in Stage 2 holds. The full value test belongs in Stage 5.

**Caveat on stated rationale.** Management's stated trigger is excess cash and debt capacity: buybacks began "after management determined that the Company's available cash and debt capacity exceeded the funds required." It does not frame the decision as price versus value. In practice the results have been price-sensible, but the rule they state is not the framework's rule.

**Inconsistency: shares issued for an acquisition.** In 1985 the company issued about 7.1M treasury shares, valued at about $168.6M or roughly $23.83 a share, to help pay for Embassy Communications, a TV production business. In the same year it bought back 15M shares at about $25.33. The 1982 letter warns about issuing shares on terms that give owners less business value than they give up (`brk-letter-1982#17`). Handing over shares of a franchise earning 20%+ unlevered, at about 14x earnings, for a television producer looks like such a transaction.

**Debt-funded buybacks.** The 1987 buybacks were financed largely with short-term borrowings, which rose by about $1.2B. Buying an undervalued franchise with modest borrowing is defensible. However, management's plan to raise the ceiling to 35% net debt to capital in order to "reduce the weighted average cost of capital" runs against the framework's preference for little or no debt. The 1977 article criticizes companies that "heap on debt" (`fortune-1977-inflation#6`). This is the leverage flag carried from Stage 1. At 15.3% net it is acceptable; at 35% it would not fit the framework well.

**Verdict on 4.2:** passes. Shareholder-friendly repurchases have been demonstrated over four years, at prices mostly well below what this business appears to be worth. The two marks against are the 1985 share issuance and a debt-capacity rationale rather than a value rationale.

## 4.3 Each $1 retained must create at least $1 of market value

The test: "whether retention, over time, delivers shareholders at least $1 of market value for each $1 retained" (`brk-letter-1983`). It is measured per share, so buybacks are included automatically.

**1982–1987, split-adjusted per share**
- Net income: 1.32 + 1.37 + 1.59 + 1.84 + 2.42 + 2.43 = **$10.97**
- Dividends: 0.83 + 0.89 + 0.92 + 0.99 + 1.04 + 1.12 cash + 0.90 in-kind (CPE shares) = **$6.69**
- Retained: **$4.28**
- Market price: $11.58 at the end of 1981 to $38.13 at the end of 1987, a gain of **$26.55**
- Ratio: **about $6.20 of market value per $1 retained**

**Stripping out the market's re-rating:**
- Holding the 1981 multiple (about 8.9x) on 1987 EPS gives about $21.63.
- That is a gain of about $10.05 against $4.28 retained, **about 2.3 to 1**.
- The test passes even if the market had never paid a higher multiple. To today's $46.56 the raw ratio is higher still.

**Where the retained capital went.** The aggregate result is excellent, but it is driven by the concentrate core. Some uses of capital look weaker:

| Use of capital | Evidence | Return |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrate (core) | $1.43B pre-tax operating income on $4.17B of identifiable assets | ~34% pre-tax, ~52% internationally |
| Entertainment, 1982–87 | 1986: $235M operating income on $2,170M of assets | ~11% pre-tax, below management's own 12% after-tax hurdle |
| Equity stakes (CCE, CPE, TCC, others; ~$2.26B carried) | 1987 equity income $118.5M | ~5% pre-tax |
| CCE specifically | Company share $43M on $749M carried | ~5.8% |
| Foods | $67M operating income on $627M of assets ($103M before provision) | 11–16% pre-tax |

- **Entertainment** was a diversification that earned less than the company's own cost of capital. Management has now pulled back: the business was combined into CPE, a 34.1M-share in-kind dividend was paid, and the company holds 49%. Whether owners gained or lost over the full holding period cannot be measured, because the 1982 purchase price of the studio is not in the packet.
- **Bottler stakes** are low-return on their own reported earnings. Management's defense is strategic: stronger, better-capitalized bottlers drive concentrate volume, and the core economics in Stage 2 support that view. Public stakes had a market value only about $44M above carrying value at the end of 1987.
- **Dividend policy.** Management plans to cut the payout to 40% to "reinvest more substantially in opportunities offering returns in excess of the cost of capital." This logic matches the framework. But the concentrate business needs little capital, so additional retained earnings will go either to buybacks (good when below value) or to acquisitions and equity stakes (where this team's record is mixed). The 1987 report's commitment to "remain highly selective… preferring investments within our existing lines of business" moves in the right direction.

**Verdict on 4.3:** passes comfortably in aggregate. The weak spots are entertainment, which has been largely reversed, and low-return bottler stakes, which are strategic and should be watched.

## Compensation (no specific framework rule; noted for completeness)

- A 3M-share restricted stock plan; 178,500 shares granted in 1987.
- An 8M-share 1987 option plan; 5.6M options outstanding (about 1.5% of shares); about 1.3M exercised per year, against 14M repurchased.
- Performance units on 555,000 shares paying cash for any rise above $20.63, the January 1985 price. At $46.56 this is roughly $14M of value.
- These rewards are tied to stock price, with no charge for capital retained or borrowed. Dilution is modest and far outweighed by buybacks.
- The 1983 owner-manager principles value directors who are "all major shareholders" (`brk-letter-1983#0`). The packet does not show director or officer holdings.

## What the investor would have relied on that the packet lacks

1. **Meeting the chairman and president directly.** The 1987 letter says purchases target managers who are "high-grade, talented and likeable" (`brk-letter-1987#18`), and the framework puts heavy weight on directly judging character and intent. Key questions: Is price versus value the real buyback trigger? How far will they push the 35% debt ceiling? Is the entertainment chapter closed? Would they welcome a large, long-term holder?
2. **1988 capital actions.** The pace and prices of the 40M-share program in 1988, when the stock traded between $35 and $44; any 1988 acquisitions or bottler stakes; actual 1988 debt levels.
3. **The proxy statement.** CEO and officer pay formulas, insider and director share ownership, and board independence on capital decisions.
4. **The 1982 entertainment purchase price and the CPE market value,** to judge the full-cycle return on that diversification.
5. **Bottler investment plans,** to know how much future retained capital will go into ~5%-return equity stakes as opposed to the core business.

---

## Stage 3 conclusion

The managers are able operators. Their buybacks of about 12% of the company at an average of about $28 a share show, in the framework's sense, that they will act in shareholders' interest. Retained earnings have produced about $2.30 to $6.20 of market value per dollar, depending on whether the re-rating is counted.

The marks against them are:
- An entertainment diversification that earned below their own hurdle, partly paid for with shares issued at about 14x earnings.
- Some promotional and financial-engineering tone in the reports.
- A stated willingness to raise leverage to 35% net debt to capital.

The direction of travel, toward focus on concentrate, repurchases and selectivity, is the right one. Management does not disqualify the company. What remains open is the information that only a direct conversation would supply. The analysis proceeds to valuation, carrying a watch item on leverage and on how future retained capital is deployed.
