**Verdict on Rule 2: passes clearly.** The evidence is share, margin, pricing power, resilience to a self-inflicted error, and a long international runway, all from the packet. The moat is the brand plus the distribution system, and management is widening it.

## §3 Rule 3: Owner earnings

Per `brk-letter-1986`: reported earnings, plus depreciation, amortization and other non-cash charges, less the average annual capitalized expenditure for plant and equipment.

|---|---|

| + Depreciation | 151.9 |

I leave out the $273M working-capital release in 1987. It flatters cash from operations and the formula as quoted does not include it.

### The capex problem

The packet does not split maintenance capex from growth capex.
- Total capex (1987: $300M) is about 2× depreciation ($152M).
- Some of it is plainly not maintenance: corporate building and computer spending ($74M), and international expansion that management calls heavy investment.
- Using the three-year average capex of $353M (1985–87) gives the most conservative figure.

### Owner earnings range (372.4M shares)

| Basis | $M | Per share |
|---|---|---|
| Average capex $353M, cash dividends from affiliates only | ≈ 606 | **$1.63** |
| 1987 capex $300M, cash dividends from affiliates only | ≈ 659 | **$1.77** |
| Plus undistributed share of affiliates' earnings (look-through) | ≈ 739 | $1.98 |
| If maintenance capex ≈ depreciation, no look-through | ≈ 807 | $2.17 |
| If maintenance capex ≈ depreciation, plus look-through | ≈ 887 | $2.38 |

Reported EPS was $2.43. **Owner earnings fall somewhere between roughly $1.65 and $2.40 per share.** The bottom of the range treats all capex as required to stand still, which is too harsh for a business described as capital-light. The top treats affiliates' retained earnings as fully worth their face value, which is generous given their 2–6% returns.

A working central estimate is **about $2.00 per share**. Two caveats:
- It includes whatever 1987 currency strength contributed. If the dollar returns to 1985 levels, that could cost something on the order of $0.25.
- It contains no 1988 data.

## Facts the framework would want that the packet lacks

1. **1988 results**, interim or full-year. This is the largest gap. The latest earnings are 14 months old at the as-of date.
2. **Capex split** between maintenance and growth, by segment. This is needed to narrow owner earnings.
3. **Constant-currency results**: the actual translation effect on operating income each year.
4. **Price and mix vs. volume**: concentrate price per unit over time, by region. This is direct evidence of pricing power.
5. **Competitor economics**: the primary competitor's margins and international share.
6. **Bottler economics**: full CCE returns and debt-service capacity, and the terms of the discretionary pricing. Together these show whether earnings are being moved between the parent and the affiliate.
7. **Contract terms** with major fountain accounts (length, exclusivity).
8. **Current-cost income figure**, which management says is lower but does not quantify.
9. **Puerto Rico tax exemption** after 1995: what the tax rate does when it expires.

## Stage 2 conclusion

The core soft drink business passes every §3 test:
- **Return on capital:** about 20% after tax on all capital for a decade, and about 34% pretax on the segment's identifiable assets.
- **Moat:** durable, with demonstrated resilience to a major error, and being widened.
- **Owner earnings:** strong in absolute terms, around $2.00 per share on a central estimate.

The weaknesses are peripheral or matters of measurement:
- Foods has no moat;
- the equity stakes earn low returns;
- currency flatters recent growth;
- off-balance-sheet guarantees and related-party pricing are flags to watch.

None of these touches the franchise itself.

**Handed forward:**
- **§4 (management and capital allocation):** test the 40M-share buyback and the 35% net-debt ceiling against intrinsic value, and apply the $1-retained / $1-of-value test.
- **§5 (valuation):** use the owner-earnings range of about $1.65–$2.40 against the $46.56 price, while recognizing that 1988 is missing.
