# Stage 3: Management and capital allocation

I apply the three rules in framework section 4 in order: honest, able management in place with candid reporting; buybacks only well below intrinsic value; and the $1-retained test. I add the related share-issuance standard from `brk-letter-1982#17`.

---

## 4.1 Honest, able management already in place; candid reporting (`brk-letter-1979`, `brk-letter-1983#2`)

**In place.** Goizueta (Chairman and CEO) and Keough (President and COO) have run the company together since 1981, and the 1987 letter says the team was "assembled" then. There is depth below them:
- Ivester as CFO
- Halle running International
- Herbert running North America
- a new Foods head brought in after the 1987 restructuring

Berkshire would not need to supply management here, which meets criterion #4 in `brk-letter-1986#10`.

**Ability: what changed under this team (1981 → 1988)**

| Measure | 1981 | 1988 |
|---|---|---|
| Return on equity | ~20% | 33% (about 24% unlevered, per Stage 2) |
| EPS, continuing operations | $1.17 | $2.85 (~13.5% a year) |
| World soft-drink share | <38% | ~45% |
| Servings per day | 359M | 559M |
| Share of operating income from the core franchise | diluted by plastics, pasta, wine-era assets, film | >90% soft drinks |

They also restructured a fragmented US bottling system:
- CCE now handles about 45% of US volume.
- Johnston and Consolidated have been recapitalized.
- The US system was moved from syrup to concentrate, which is cheaper to ship.

German bottlers were consolidated from 102 to 64. These moves strengthen the moat that Stage 2 identified.

**Candor: mixed, mostly good**

On the positive side:
- The reports separate out unusual items every year. The 1987 letter gives growth "excluding 35 cents per share of unusual items in 1986."
- The 1986 report says "we were not satisfied with the performance of Columbia Pictures' feature films."
- The 1987 report says Foods "is obviously in transition."
- The 1988 report states its weak points:
  - frozen orange juice volume down 22%
  - Brazil and Argentina declining
  - a possible $175M equity hit from FASB 96
  - guarantees and recourse receivables
- Management publishes its 12% cost of capital and its 35% debt ceiling. Owners can therefore judge its decisions against its own stated hurdle.

On the negative side, parts of the reports read like the "public relations document" the framework objects to:
- a claim that the system "added an estimated $250 billion to the world economy" using a multiplier of five
- 1987 self-congratulation on "creative, decisive actions"
- a recurring emphasis on stock-price performance

The entertainment exit is presented as an "innovative business structure," not as a mistake. `brk-letter-1985#6` is the opposite approach: studying errors openly.

**Verdict on 4.1: passes.** The managers are able, owner-oriented, and largely candid. Their reporting has a promotional streak.

## 4.2 Capital allocation record

**(a) Diversification outside the moat, now mostly reversed**

Entertainment:
- Columbia Pictures was bought in 1982. Its price is not in the packet.
- Embassy and Tandem followed in 1985, for about $445M.
- Merv Griffin followed in 1986, for about $200M.
- The entertainment business (EBS) earned net income of $109M in 1985, $102M in 1986 and $51M in 1987. Its net assets were $1.33B, so it earned roughly 8% on them, below management's own 12% hurdle.
- In 1987 it was merged into CPE. 34.1M CPE shares were paid out to Coca-Cola shareholders as a $335M dividend in kind, and Coca-Cola kept 49%.
- CPE then wrote down $114M, and Coca-Cola's carrying value fell from $989M to $598M.
- The cash outcome was not a disaster. Management says it "recovered virtually all" of the Embassy investment in 1986, and Wheel of Fortune and Jeopardy! were profitable.
- But this is retained capital that earned below the cost of capital. It is the kind of "adrenalin-soaked" acquisition activity `brk-letter-1983#3` warns about.

Other non-core businesses have been sold:
- plastics (Presto and Winkler), sold in 1985 for $112M
- Ronco pasta, sold in 1984
- coffee and tea, sold in January 1989

The 1987 statement of intent, "preferring investments within our existing lines of business", points the right way.

**(b) Share issuance (`brk-letter-1982#17`)**

- In 1985 the company issued 7.1M treasury shares for Embassy. They were valued at about $168.6M, or about $23.8 a share. That is roughly 14× 1985 EPS, paid to buy into a weaker business.
- At today's $46.56, those shares are worth about $330M.
- There is no other significant stock issuance apart from option exercises. That still counts against the "issue only when we receive as much as we give" standard, though it is small relative to the buybacks.

**(c) Bottler investments**

- The company buys bottlers and resells them to CCE at carrying value (Rainwater, Miami, Memphis), with no losses.
- It takes 20–49% stakes so that leveraged bottling sits in separate companies.
- CCE's own returns are low: about 8% on equity in 1988, including a one-time gain. These are not high-return investments in themselves.
- The rationale is defensible as spending to keep the franchise strong: "the strength of The Coca-Cola Company depends in large part on the strength of our bottling partners."
- Public equity investees trade $291M above carrying value (end-1988).

**(d) Dividend policy**

- The payout has been cut deliberately: 65% in 1983, 58% in 1984, 42% in 1988, with a 40% target.
- The stated reason is "to reinvest more substantially in opportunities offering returns in excess of the cost of capital."
- Retention is the right choice only if the retained money earns well. Section 4.4 tests that.

**(e) Leverage**

- Management raised its debt ceiling to 35% "to lower the weighted average cost of capital." Debt plus cash then funded buybacks.
- Net debt is modest at about one year's net income, but leverage now produces about 9 points of the reported ROE.

## 4.3 Buybacks only well below intrinsic value (`brk-letter-1984`)

Price per share is cost ÷ shares from the equity statements. EPS is that year's reported EPS.

| Year | Shares bought | Cost | Avg price | P/E paid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1984 | 18.4M | $335.7M | ~$18.2 | ~11.6× |
| 1985 | 15.0M | $379.9M | ~$25.3 | ~14.7× |
| 1986 | 2.9M | $110.5M | ~$38.4 | ~18.5× (on ~$2.07 ex-items) |
| 1987 | 14.2M | $605.1M | ~$42.7 | ~17.6× |
| 1988 | 19.0M | $758.2M | ~$39.9 | ~14.0× |
| **Total** | **69.4M** | **$2,189M** | **~$31.5** | |

- Average shares fell from 408M (1983) to 364.6M (1988), about 11%. Shares outstanding at year-end 1988 were 354.8M.
- 14.4M shares remain under the 40M-share program through 1990.
- Every year's purchases were made below today's $46.56, and the 1984–85 purchases far below it.
- The 1987 purchases at about $42.7 (17.6×) include some bought near that year's highs (Q3 1987 ranged $42.88–53.13). Those are the least clearly "well below" value.
- Management states its rationale as cash and debt capacity in excess of operating needs, continuing "as long as investing in our own stock remains one of the best uses of our excess cash."
  - It compares alternative uses of cash, which is sound.
  - It never says it buys because the price is below intrinsic value. Price discipline is not explicitly stated.

**Verdict on 4.3: provisional pass.** Whether purchases at 12–18× earnings were "well below intrinsic value" depends on the Stage 4 valuation. Given Stage 2's owner earnings and growth, they very likely were. For a prospective holder, a continuing buyback raises our ownership share over time without our putting in more money.

## 4.4 Each $1 retained should create at least $1 of market value (`brk-letter-1983`)

**Since this team took over (end-1980 to end-1988)**

Market value:
- End-1980: $11.13 × ~372M shares ≈ $4.1B. I use the 1980 average share count, because the year-end count is not in the packet.
- End-1988: $44.63 × 354.8M ≈ $15.8B.
- Increase: **≈ $11.7B**.

Retained earnings, 1981–88:
- Net income to common: about $5.79B
- Cash dividends: about $2.99B
- CPE dividend in kind: $0.34B
- Retained: **≈ $2.5B**

Result: about **$4.70 of market value per $1 retained**.

The test is conservative here in two ways. First, $2.19B more went out as buybacks, which shrink market value. Second, the CPE shares already distributed carry their own market value.

**Removing the re-rating.** The P/E rose from about 10.5× (1980) to about 15.7× (1988), and part of the market-value gain comes from that rise. If 1988 earnings are valued at the 1980 multiple, market value would be about $11B. The gain would then be about $6.9B, or still about $2.70 per $1 retained.

**Shorter window (end-1983 to end-1988)**

- Market value rose from about $7.1B to $15.8B.
- Retained earnings were about $1.9B.
- That is about $4.60 per $1 retained.

**Verdict on 4.4: passes by a wide margin**, despite the below-hurdle entertainment money.

## 4.5 Compensation and alignment

The packet shows only the plan mechanics.

**Restricted stock**
- 3M shares are authorized. Grants were 883,500 in 1984, then 282,000 / 210,000 / 178,500, then 511,500 in 1988.
- The 1988 terms add an exception to forfeiture for "a change of control in the Company," which is a mildly entrenching feature.

**Options**
- 8.66M are outstanding (about 2.4% of shares), at exercise prices of $10–45.
- Grants jumped to 4.18M in 1988, from 1.1–1.8M a year earlier.
- Options are issued at market price, so they reward the stock price rising over time, not performance above the 12% hurdle. In effect they are share issuance; see 4.2(b).

**Cash units**
- Performance units on 555,000 shares are measured from a $20.63 base (1985).
- Incentive units on 300,000 shares were added in 1988.

**Missing:** the proxy statement figures for executive pay and for what Goizueta, Keough, and the directors own personally. The framework places weight on owner-managers who "eat our own cooking" (`brk-letter-1983#0`). I cannot test that from this packet.

## What the investor would have relied on that the packet lacks

1. **Meeting and judging the people.** The framework's permanent holdings rest on managers "we like and admire" (`brk-letter-1987#18`), and in the Mrs. B example a handshake replaced an audit (`brk-letter-1984#7`). Personal assessment of Goizueta's and Keough's character, temperament, and attitude toward a large shareholder is not in the packet. Whether the investor knew them personally isn't in the packet either.
2. **The proxy statement:** pay levels, insider ownership, and option terms in detail.
3. **How management decides on buybacks:** whether it estimates intrinsic value or simply spends excess cash. A direct conversation would settle this.
4. **The full Columbia Pictures history:** the 1982 price and cumulative returns, to see the true cost of the diversification episode and whether management now views it as a lesson.
5. **Returns on bottler investments** at the Coca-Cola level, and how conflicts are handled. Coca-Cola sets concentrate prices to a company it owns 49% of.
6. **Succession plans and the board's independence and engagement.**
7. **The 1988 10-Qs and 10-K, and the ability to ask questions** about country profitability and the split between maintenance and growth capex (carried over from Stage 2).

## Stage 3 conclusion

The managers are able and owner-oriented, and they have already shown they create far more than $1 of market value for every $1 retained. They have refocused capital on a high-return franchise, cut the payout, sold non-core businesses, and repurchased about 11% of the shares at prices well below today's.

The negatives are real but bounded:
- an entertainment diversification that earned below their own hurdle, now largely separated off
- one share issuance at about 14× earnings
- debt-funded buybacks that raised leverage
- a large 1988 option grant with a change-of-control clause
- a promotional tone in parts of the reports

None of these disqualifies the company. The questions carried into the valuation stage are whether buybacks continue only below value, and whether "opportunistic" planning stays inside the soft-drink business. The pay and ownership figures are unknown and would have been checked directly.
