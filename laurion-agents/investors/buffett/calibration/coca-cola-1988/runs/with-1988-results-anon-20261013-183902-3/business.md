## Stage 2 conclusion

The business passes all three tests in section 3.

- **Return on equity (3a).** It passes on the framework's own terms. Returns were above 20% in every year since 1978, including the near debt-free years of 1978–81. After removing leverage and one-time items, the business earns about 25% on all net capital. The concentrate business alone earns about 44% pretax on its assets. The headline 33% is about 8 points higher than that because of debt-funded buybacks, so I'd treat 25–28% as the sustainable figure for the whole company.
- **Moat (3b).** It is real and widening. Worldwide share rose from under 38% to nearly 45% over nine years, gross margins went up, and the core business needs little capital. The cost of keeping it is about $842M a year of advertising, and it depends on bottlers that earn low returns.
- **Owner earnings (3c).** Roughly $2.3–$2.9 per share, against $2.85 reported. The midpoint is about $2.5–$2.6. Reported earnings are a fair proxy for cash earnings, with a modest discount for capital spending.

**Items carried to later stages:**
1. **Management (Stage 4).** Leverage is elective and used for buybacks. The company diversified into entertainment in 1982–87 and has since retreated. It keeps putting capital into low-return bottler stakes. The reports' tone is promotional, but they also disclose one-time items candidly.
2. **Valuation (Stage 5).** Use owner earnings of about $2.3–$2.9 per share, not the 33% ROE. Account separately for currency and for the roughly $1.9B in affiliates, worth about $291M above book.
3. **Risks to weigh, not to exclude on.** Currency translation reversing, very high inflation in Latin American markets, and the bottlers' financial health.
