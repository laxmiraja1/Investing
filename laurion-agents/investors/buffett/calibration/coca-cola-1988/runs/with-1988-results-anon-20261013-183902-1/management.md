# Stage 3: Management and capital allocation (framework section 4)

The section has three rules, applied here in order: honest and able management already in place with candid reporting; buybacks only well below intrinsic value; and each $1 retained must create at least $1 of market value. Acquisitions, share issuance, dividends, leverage and compensation are covered as evidence under those rules. Figures are $ millions unless stated.

## 4a. Honest, able management in place; candid reporting

**Ability, judged from the operating record.** The chairman/CEO and the president have run the company together since 1981 (`ar-1987` letter: "When we assembled our present management team in 1981…"). Over that period:

| | ~1981 | 1988 |
|---|---|---|
| Worldwide share | <38% | ~45% |
| Daily servings | 359M | 559M |
| ROE | ~20–22% | 33% |
| Soft-drink pretax return on assets | n/a | 44% (36% in 1986) |
| EPS from continuing operations | $1.17 | $2.85 |

Management also accepted operating discipline at some short-term cost. In 1986 it cut U.S. marketing spending after the heavy 1985 launches, closed syrup plants as bottlers moved to concentrate, and ran Latin America for margin rather than volume. The managers who built this record are in place, which satisfies the requirement "management in place (we can't supply it)."

**Owner orientation in stated policy.**
- The company states its primary objective as "increase shareholder value."
- It publishes a hurdle rate: about 12% after-tax cost of capital for soft-drink-like investments (`ar-1986`, `ar-1987`, `ar-1988` MD&A).
- It explicitly reports per-share results: "our aggressive share repurchase program heightened profit growth for the individual shareholder" (`ar-1988`).

These are the right yardsticks. Talk about "shareholder value" proves nothing by itself, though. `brk-letter-1984#4` warns that it was "this season's favorite" phrase. The evidence that counts is behavior, covered under 4b and 4c.

**Candor.** Mixed, but adequate.

Points in favor:
- One-time items are separated rather than buried. The 1986 letter discloses the $375M IPO gain and the $180M provisions with their per-share effect. The 1987 letter gives growth excluding "35 cents per share of unusual items."
- The film studio's poor year is admitted: "we were not satisfied with the performance of the film studio's feature films" (`ar-1986`). Foods is described as "obviously in transition" (`ar-1987`).
- Several unflattering items are disclosed: the effect of currency on results; that current-cost income "would be less than reported"; contingent guarantees; CPE receivables sold with recourse; and a possible $175M hit to equity from FASB 96.

Points against:
- Much of the report is promotional. Examples are the multiplier claim of "$250 billion to the world economy," the anecdote pages, and repeated emphasis on stock-price total return versus the S&P.
- The 1986 letter leads with "EPS +41%," a figure inflated by the IPO gain.
- Entertainment was praised in 1986 as "this industry's innovative leader." It was spun out in 1987, and the 1988 write-downs came with no stated reckoning of what entertainment earned on the capital put into it.

`brk-letter-1979` says "we don't expect a public relations document." Parts of these reports are exactly that. The financial sections underneath them are straightforward, however, and nothing in the packet suggests the numbers are misleading.

## 4b. Buybacks only well below intrinsic value

The framework treats buybacks as the clearest test of whether management acts for owners (`brk-letter-1984#4`). Here is the record from the shareholders'-equity statements:

| Year | Shares bought (M) | Cost | Avg price | P/E on that year's EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1984 | 18.4 | 336 | $18.24 | 11.6x |
| 1985 | 15.0 | 380 | $25.33 | 14.7x |
| 1986 | 2.9 | 110 | $38.37 | 15.9x (≈18.5x ex-items) |
| 1987 | 14.2 | 605 | $42.70 | 17.6x |
| 1988 | 19.0 | 758 | $39.94 | 14.0x |
| **Total** | **69.4** | **2,189** | **$31.53** | |

**Assessment.**
- **Scale.** Average shares fell from 396M (1984) to 365M (1988). The 1987–90 authorization of 40M shares is more than 10% of the company. This is a "demonstrated" pattern, not a token gesture.
- **Prices paid.** At $31.53 on average, the repurchased shares are worth about $3.2B at the last close of $46.56, for a market gain of about $1.0B. The 1988 purchases at about 14x earnings bought a business earning about 29% after tax on unlevered operating assets and growing earnings in the mid-teens. That looks well below value, but the full test belongs to Stage 5.
- **The 1987 purchases are the weakest.** They were made near the pre-crash highs ($42–53 in the third quarter) and partly with short-term borrowing (short-term debt rose $1.2B in 1987).
- **Stated rationale.** Management describes buybacks as the use of cash and debt capacity that "exceeded the funds required," and as continuing "as long as investing in our own stock remains one of the best uses of our excess cash" (`ar-1988`). That is about how cash is allocated, not explicitly about price relative to value. The framework wants buying because the price is well below value, and the packet does not show whether management formally makes that comparison.

**Share issuance** (`brk-letter-1982#17`). In 1985 the company issued about 7.1M shares, valued at about $23.75 each (around 14x earnings), as part of the payment for Embassy Communications. It later repurchased stock at about $40. Giving up a share of this franchise for an entertainment asset at that price did not give "as much in business value as we give." It is a clear mark against, although small at about 2% of shares.

## 4c. Each $1 retained should create at least $1 of market value

**Test from the start of 1981 (management's tenure) to the as-of date:**
- Cumulative net income, 1981–88: about **$5,800**.
- Cash dividends: about **$2,985**. 1984–88 come from the statements; 1981–83 are estimated from the reported payout ratios.
- In-kind CPE dividend: **$335**.
- Earnings retained: about **$2,480**.
- Market value at end-1980 was about $11.13 × 372M = **$4.14B**. At 1989-02-24 it was about $46.56 × 354.8M = **$16.52B**. The increase is about **$12.4B**, even after shares were retired.

That is roughly **$5 of market value per $1 retained**. Two caveats apply:
1. Part of the gain is a higher market multiple: about 10.5x earnings in 1980 against 16.3x now.
2. Part is financed by about $1.0B more net debt.

Even after subtracting the extra debt and allowing for the re-rating, the result passes comfortably. On the business itself, net income rose about $563M a year on about $2.5B retained plus about $1.0B of added net debt. That is an incremental return near 16–20%, partly helped by a weaker dollar.

**Where the retained dollars went, and how each use scores:**

| Use | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Core soft-drink capex** | About $240M in 1988 against $1,691M of soft-drink operating income | Excellent. Little capital needed for very high returns. |
| **Buybacks** | $2.2B since 1984 | Good to very good, subject to the Stage 5 value test. |
| **Entertainment** (studio 1982; Embassy/Tandem 1985 for $178M cash plus stock; Merv Griffin 1986, ~$200M) | Operating income rose more than 30% a year while owned. Then combined into CPE, 51% distributed, and the remaining 49% carried at $598M plus $100M of preferred stock. CPE lost money in 1988 ($114M write-down; company share −$51M for Dec–Feb). | Poor. It moved capital into a business without the core's moat and paid partly in undervalued stock. Credit for reversing the move and returning to the core ("more than 90 percent of our 1988 operating income"). |
| **Bottler investments** (CCE 49%, TCC 49%, UK JV 49%, Consolidated 20–30%, Johnston 22%, plus $167M CCFC loans to Johnston) | About $1.9B in affiliates produced $48M of equity income in 1988 (2.5%) and $113M in 1987 (5%). Market value of the public investees exceeds carrying value by about $291M. | Low direct return, but a strategic defense of the concentrate franchise's distribution. Holding exactly 49% also keeps about $2.9B of CCE liabilities off the balance sheet. Watch item. |
| **Foods** (Nutri-Foods $30M; Hood citrus $45M; coffee sold 1989) | About 13% pretax on assets, a restructuring, and a management change | Small, mediocre. Management is narrowing it rather than expanding it. |
| **Leverage policy** | Net-debt ceiling of 35% "to lower its overall cost of capital"; $300M money-market preferred issued | Moderate, but the framework wants returns "without undue leverage." Net debt is under one year's earnings, so acceptable. Debt is not needed by the business. |
| **Dividends** | Payout cut from about 65% (1983) to a 40% target, in order to retain more | Justified only because the extra retention has gone to buybacks at sensible prices. The core cannot absorb it. |

**Compensation.** The framework has no specific rule on this, so it is noted as fact only:
- Options outstanding rose from 5.6M to 8.66M in 1988, about 2.4% of shares. 4.2M were granted that year.
- Restricted stock, stock appreciation rights, performance units (base $20.63) and incentive units are all tied to the share price.
- Roughly 1M shares are exercised each year, which offsets only a small part of the buybacks.
- Salaries, bonus formulas and insider ownership are in the proxy statement, which is not in the packet.

## What the investor would rely on that the packet lacks

1. **Direct judgment of the managers.** `brk-letter-1987#18` asks for "high-grade, talented and likeable managers," and `brk-letter-1986#10` describes judging "the quality of the people we will be joining." That comes from meeting and knowing them, which the packet cannot supply.
2. **How management decides on buybacks.** Does it compare price with an estimate of per-share value, or simply spend surplus cash? Would it stop buying at a high price?
3. **Intentions on future diversification.** After the entertainment experience, would management buy outside soft drinks again?
4. **The proxy statement**: executive pay, bonus metrics, insider and director share ownership, and board independence.
5. **Bottler relationships**, and whether the 49% structures serve the franchise or mainly the presentation of the balance sheet.
6. **Succession** below the CEO and president, and depth in the international operating groups that produce about 79% of soft-drink operating income.
7. **Reputation checks** with bottlers and counterparties, the kind of reference checking `brk-letter-1986#10` invites.

## Summary

The operators are first-rate and in place, with an owner-oriented scorecard of per-share results, a cost-of-capital hurdle and large buybacks. On the framework's market-value test, retained earnings have produced about $5 of value per $1 retained. The record has blemishes: the entertainment detour, financed partly by shares issued at about 14x earnings; about $1.9B parked in low-return bottling affiliates; a fondness for leverage presented as cost-of-capital engineering; and reports that are partly promotional. All of these are either being reversed or limited in size. Management passes this section. The open question is whether recent buybacks were made "well below intrinsic value," which Stage 5 must answer, along with a direct judgment of the people, which the packet cannot provide.
