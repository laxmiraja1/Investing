# Stage 2: Business analysis (Framework §3)

The three measures are applied in the order the framework gives them. Throughout, "the business" means the syrup and concentrate operation. Foods and the equity stakes are treated as appendages.

## 3.1 Return on equity, not EPS growth

The rule (`brk-letter-1979#2` context, `brk-letter-1979`) tests ROE "without undue leverage, accounting gimmickry, etc." So the reported figure has to be cleaned before it can be relied on.

**Reported record (`ar-1986`, `ar-1987` selected data; `upi-1989-01-31`):**

| | 1977 | 1979 | 1981 | 1983 | 1984 | 1985 | 1986 | 1987 | 1988 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROE (avg. equity) | 20.6% | 21.1% | 19.9% | 19.4% | 21.8% | 23.5% | 28.8% | 27.2% | 33% |
| Total debt / capital | 3.5% | 6.3% | 9.1% | 15.1% | 30.7% | 27.7% | 31.4% | 45.6% | n/a |

**Adjustment 1: leverage.** The strongest evidence is from 1976–1981. ROE held at 20–21% while debt was 3.5–9% of capital, so the business earned about 20% on equity with almost no borrowing. The recent rise in gross debt does not create the recent returns. At end-1987, net debt was about $581M:
- $2,702M total debt,
- less $1,268M excess cash,
- less $545M of entertainment-affiliate notes (paid January 1988),
- less $308M of temporary investments (sold January 1988).

Net income plus after-tax net interest (about $72M × 0.65 ≈ $47M) was about $963M. Equity plus net debt was about $3,805M. Return on total capital was therefore about **25%**, so leverage explains little of the 27%.

**Adjustment 2: one-time items.**
- 1986 includes a $262M after-tax gain on the bottler IPO and $128M of after-tax provisions. Excluding both, net income was about $800M, or about **24.6%** on average equity of $3,247M.
- 1987's $40M gain and $36M provision roughly offset.
- Management's own ex-items figures show net income up 14.5% and EPS up 17.4% in 1987 (`ar-1987` letter).

**Adjustment 3: the shrinking denominator.** Part of the 1986–88 rise in ROE is arithmetic, not better economics:
- Treasury stock bought at about 4× book grew from $604M to $1,309M.
- A $335M in-kind dividend of entertainment stock left equity.
- 1988 removed another 18.9M shares.

Whether those buybacks were wise belongs to Stage 3 (§4). For §3.1 the point is that the *level* of about 20%+ unlevered is proven over 12 years. The 33% figure for 1988 should not be extrapolated.

**Adjustment 4: the core is better than the consolidated figure.**
- About $2.26B of book equity sits in affiliates: entertainment $989M, CCE $749M, Canadian bottler $84M, other $435M. These produced only $118M of equity income in 1987 (about 5%).
- Soft drink operating income was $1,432.5M ($323.6M US plus $1,108.9M international) on $4,174M of identifiable assets. That is about 34% pre-tax on *gross* assets, and higher net of the roughly $1.4B of payables the business carries.

This matches the "judge by returns on underlying assets" logic in `brk-letter-1987#1`. The concentrate business earns returns closer to Berkshire's best owned units than to an average company.

**Verdict on 3.1:** passes clearly. The returns are high, have lasted over 12 years, and were earned without much leverage.

## 3.2 Durable competitive advantage (the moat)

The framework's example is GEICO's cost gap (`brk-letter-1986#16`). Here the moat is consumer franchise and distribution reach, not low cost. The questions are whether it is wide, whether it is widening, and whether pricing shows it.

**Width (from the packet):**
- About 44% of world soft drink volume excluding the USSR and China, with volume double the nearest competitor (`ar-1987`).
- US share above 39% in 1986, 9 points ahead of the primary competitor (`ar-1986`), and above 40% in 1988 (`upi-1989-01-31`).
- Fountain share was 59% in 1986. The company outsells its fountain rival 2-to-1 and captured about three-quarters of fountain segment growth over four years (`ar-1987`).
- The trademark scored record awareness and preference in 1986 (`ar-1986`).

**Widening:**
- The company took share in each year shown, with US volume growing faster than the industry in 1985–88.
- International volume grew 12%, 9% and 7% in 1986, 1987 and 1988.
- International per-capita consumption is only 16% of the US rate (`ar-1986` MD&A), which leaves a long runway.
- US per-capita consumption itself rose from 203 to 274 servings between 1980 and 1987.

**Survived a self-inflicted error.** The 1985 reformulation did not damage the franchise. The original formula, relaunched as the "classic" product, became the top US soft drink by 1986. A franchise that can absorb its owner's mistake is durable. This is in the spirit of `brk-letter-1987#3`: "Severe change and exceptional returns usually don't mix." The product is essentially unchanged after 100 years.

**Pricing power (the test in `brk-letter-1986#22`):**
- Gross margin on concentrate sold to US bottlers was about 63% (`ar-1986`).
- The company raised CCE's prices at its discretion in October 1986.
- Management states it "is able to adjust prices to compensate for increasing costs."
- In Latin America, operating income rose 17% in 1988 on 1% volume growth through margin increases (`upi-1989-01-31`).

**Weak spots and limits:**
- *Foods has no moat.* It is exposed to orange-solids pricing. Frozen OJ volume fell 22% in 1988, and operating income was $67M in 1987 after a $36M charge. This is a small drag, not a threat.
- *US Brand A syrup pricing is formula-based* under standard contracts with most US bottlers other than CCE (`ar-1986` MD&A). US pricing freedom is therefore narrower than the 63% margin suggests.
- *The moat must be maintained.* Media advertising was $720M in 1987, and selling expenses were about 28% of revenue.
- *International carries most of the weight.* About 74–77% of operating income comes from outside the US, in 40 currencies.

**Verdict on 3.2:** a wide and widening moat around a simple product. This is the strongest part of the case.

## 3.3 Owner earnings (`brk-letter-1986`)

The definition is (a) reported earnings, plus (b) D&A and other non-cash charges, less (c) the average capital expenditure needed to maintain the business's competitive position and volume.

**1987, from the cash flow statement (`ar-1987`):**

| Item | $M |
|---|---|
| Net income | 916.1 |
| + Depreciation & goodwill amortization | 153.5 |
| + Non-cash restructuring provision | 36.4 |
| − Deferred tax (a benefit in 1987) | (28.7) |
| − Non-cash IPO gain | (39.7) |
| (a)+(b) before retained affiliate earnings | ~1,038 |
| − Equity income not received in cash | (79.7) |
| Working capital provided by operations | 958.8 |

**Item (c), required capex, is the figure the packet does not give.** Two bounds are possible:
- **Low case.** Treat all capex as required: the 3-year average of $352M ($412M, $346M, $300M). Owner earnings ≈ $959M − $352M ≈ **$610M** (about $1.60 per share on 377M shares). This overstates (c). The spending includes expansion in China, Indonesia and Taiwan and a new office building, and asset disposals returned $124–155M a year in 1986–87.
- **Central case.** Assume maintenance capex is about depreciation ($152M), raised by roughly a third for inflation because management concedes current-cost income is below reported (`ar-1987`, "Impact of inflation"). That gives about $200M. Count retained affiliate earnings as value, since they are look-through earnings. Owner earnings ≈ $1,038M − $200M ≈ **$840M**, or about **$2.20 per share**, roughly 90% of reported EPS.

**Rough extension to 1988.** Net income was $1.04B. Assuming the same ratio, owner earnings would be about **$0.9–0.95B**, or roughly $2.55–2.70 per share on about 354M shares after the 1988 buybacks. This is an estimate, because no 1988 cash flow statement is in the packet.

**Verdict on 3.3:** reported earnings are close to real cash available to owners. The business is not a Burlington-style capital sink (`brk-letter-1985#9`). Its incremental investment goes largely into growth that earns high returns. Two items make it look less capital-light than it is:
- the 1987 working-capital release of $273M, which is non-recurring and excluded above;
- the bottler investments, which the company deliberately moved off its books. Those bottlers carry heavy debt: CCE had $2.7B of liabilities against $1.5B of net assets.

## Facts the framework would want that the packet lacks

1. **1988 annual report**: balance sheet, cash flow, capex, debt level, effective tax rate. 1988 is visible only through two wire stories.
2. **A maintenance vs. growth split of capex**, the key input to item (c).
3. **Constant-currency results.** The dollar weakened by 27%, 16% and 10% against key currencies in 1986–88, which flattered reported growth. The packet gives no figure for how much.
4. **A price/volume breakdown of concentrate revenue over a long period**, which would test pricing power directly rather than by inference.
5. **Competitor economics**: the primary rival's margins, advertising per case and ROE, to measure the moat's width the way GEICO's cost gap was measured.
6. **Bottler contract terms and bottler economics**, especially the US formula-pricing contracts and how much of the system's leverage could end up with the parent.
7. **Contingent liabilities in detail**: about $394M of bottler and finance-subsidiary guarantees, plus about $325M of recourse on entertainment receivables.
8. **Market values of the affiliate stakes in 1988.** The packet gives only the end-1987 $44M excess over carrying value, and the entertainment affiliate took write-downs in 1988.

## Stage 2 conclusion

On every §3 measure this is a superior business:
- about 20%+ return on equity sustained for 12 years with little debt, and about 25% on total capital now;
- a wide and widening franchise with demonstrated pricing power;
- owner earnings in the region of $0.85B for 1987 and probably about $0.9B+ for 1988, roughly matching reported profits.

Several questions are left for later stages:
- whether the buybacks and borrowing create value (§4);
- what the business is worth against a $17.3B market value (§5);
- how much currency effects flatter recent growth (which affects the earnings base used in valuation).
