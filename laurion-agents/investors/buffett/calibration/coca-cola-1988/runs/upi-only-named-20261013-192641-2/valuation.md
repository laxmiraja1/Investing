# Stage 4: Valuation (Framework §5) and Catalyst / timing (§6)

At $46.56, Coca-Cola fails the only valuation rule written in §5, buying below net working capital, by a factor of about 1,000. By 1989 the framework itself had replaced that rule for outstanding businesses. The test it substitutes is whether the price makes sense if the future looks much like the past, with value measured by owner earnings.

On that test the stock passes:
- If the next decade resembles the last, the purchase returns about 12–15% a year, without needing a higher P/E.
- In a weaker case, about 10% a year.
- Capitalizing owner earnings at management's own 12% cost of capital gives about **$54–67 a share**, a margin of safety of roughly **15–30%**.

This is a fair price for an outstanding business, not a Graham-style bargain. No catalyst is needed.

---

## 5.1 The framework's written rule: buy below net working capital (`bpl-letter-1965`)

Year-end 1987 balance sheet (ar-1987):

| | $M |
|---|---|
| Current assets | 4,136.2 |
| Current liabilities | 4,118.5 |
| **Net working capital** | **17.7**, about **$0.05 a share** |
| Shareholders' equity | 3,223.8 |
| Less goodwill and intangibles | −74.2 |
| Tangible book | 3,149.6, about $8.46 a share |

I estimate year-end 1988 equity at about $3.1B, or about $8.75 a share on roughly 355M shares. The packet has no 1988 balance sheet.

The price is about 5.3 times book and about 900 times net working capital. **Result: fails.**

**Why this rule doesn't decide the case.** The framework's later evidence explicitly sets aside the bargain test for businesses like this one:
- "Our goal is to find an outstanding business at a sensible price, not a mediocre business at a bargain price," followed by Buffett calling his 20 years of bargain-hunting a costly education (`brk-letter-1987#18`).
- "A good business purchased at a fair price" beats "a poor business purchased at a bargain price" (`brk-letter-1979`).
- The test: "prices that will make sense, even should the future of the acquired enterprise develop much along the lines of its past." Also: "We may very well pay a fairly fancy price for a Category 1 business if we are reasonably confident of what we are getting" (`brk-letter-1981#3`).
- Value is measured by owner earnings, "the relevant item for valuation purposes" (`brk-letter-1986`), and by value to a private owner (`bpl-letter-1963#5`).

So I value the business on owner earnings and apply the "past continues" test. I use no discount-rate formula beyond what the packet itself supplies.

## 5.3 Assets valued separately

**Investments.** The holdings are CCE (49%, carried at $749M), T.C.C. ($84M), other bottlers ($435M) and CPE (49%, carried at $989M at end-1987 and written down in 1988 by an amount not disclosed). The company put the market value of its bottling and entertainment investments at over $2.3B at end-1987, and the public stakes were worth only $44M above carrying value (ar-1987). I take about $1.8B, a roughly 20% haircut for the CPE write-downs. That is about **$5 a share**.

**Net debt.** This is not subtracted separately. Net debt was 15.3% of capital at end-1987, and its interest cost is already netted inside core net income (net interest expense fell 52% in 1988).

## 5.4 The "past continues" test (`brk-letter-1981#3`)

**What the past looks like** (ar-1987 selected data, restated continuing EPS):

| Period | Per-share earnings growth |
|---|---|
| 1978–1988 ($0.95 → $2.85) | 11.6% a year |
| 1983–1988 ($1.35 → $2.85) | 16.1% a year |
| 1989 management target | "Mid-teens" (not relied on) |

The 1986–88 years were helped by a weaker dollar (+27%, +16%, +10%) and by buybacks financed with debt. I therefore use **12%** as the base case, between the 10-year and 5-year rates, and **10%** as the weaker case.

**Ten-year look forward.** I buy at $46.56, collect dividends (starting at $1.20 and growing with earnings), and sell at the end of 1998. The exit multiple is no higher than today's.

| Case | Owner earnings 1998 | Exit multiple | Exit price | Dividends received | Annual return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base: $2.47, growing 12% | $7.67 | 15× (below today's 19×) | $115 | about $23.6 | **about 12.6%** |
| Base, same multiple as today | $7.67 | 19× | $146 | about $23.6 | **about 15%** |
| Weaker: $2.24, growing 10% | $5.81 | 15× | $87 | about $19 | **about 10%** |
| Stress: $2.24, growing 5% | $3.65 | 15× | $55 | about $15 | about 4–5% |

**What these show.**
- If the business performs as it has, the return is about 12–15%. That needs neither a higher multiple nor any help from the market.
- In the weaker case the return is about 10%.
- Even in the stress case, where growth halves, capital is not lost, which is §9's main concern.
- A bad outcome requires growth to collapse to about 5% for a decade. The record argues against that: per-share earnings rose every year from 1977 to 1988, and international consumption per person is only 16% of the U.S. level.

**Capitalized value, as a cross-check.** I discount at management's own 12% after-tax cost of capital (ar-1986, ar-1987). I assume 10 years of growth at the stated rate, then a perpetual rate, then add $5 a share of investments.

| Case | Inputs | Value per share |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | $2.24 owner earnings; 10% for 10 years; then 4% | **$50** |
| Middle | $2.47; 10% for 10 years; then 4% | **$54** |
| Base | $2.47; 12% for 10 years; then 5% | **$67** |
| Bond-rate (assumption, see below) | $2.47; 12% for 10 years; then 4%; discounted at 9% | about $100 |

The long Treasury yield is not in the packet. I assume about 9% for the bond-rate case and the comparison below, and I do not rely on that case for the decision.

## 5.5 Margin of safety against the hurdle

**What the hurdle is.** As of this date the framework sets no fixed percentage discount for outstanding businesses. The required test is a sensible or fair price that works if the past continues. A second, implicit test is that the expected return should clearly beat a long government bond, assumed here at about 9%.

| Measure | Value | Price $46.56 vs value |
|---|---|---|
| Net working capital (§5 rule as written) | $0.05 | Fails |
| Conservative owner-earnings value | $50 | about 7% below |
| Middle | $54 | **about 14% below** |
| Base | $67 | **about 30% below** |
| Bond-rate case | about $100 | more than 50% below (relies on an assumed rate) |
| Expected return, past continues | 12.6–15% | 3.5–6 points above the assumed bond rate |
| Expected return, weaker case | about 10% | about equal to the bond, with a growing franchise behind it |

**Verdict: the hurdle is met.** The price makes sense on the past continuing, without needing a higher multiple. The numerical cushion is moderate, about 15–30% on the central estimates. Most of the real margin of safety comes from how reliable the franchise is, not from cheapness. That is the setting the framework describes as "an extremely high probability that our facts and reasoning are correct" (`bpl-letter-1965`).

**How Mr. Market has priced it.** The P/E today is about 16× trailing earnings. That is roughly where it was at end-1985 (16.4×), end-1987 (15.7×) and end-1988 (15.7×). It is well below the Q3 1987 peak (about 22× at $53). Earnings are up about 17% since that peak, yet the price is lower. The stock is not being bid up in a frenzy (`brk-letter-1986`: "fearful when others are greedy"), but it is also not a panic price. The 52-week low of $35.04 was about 25% cheaper.

## 6. Catalyst / timing

| §6 rule | Application |
|---|---|
| **No catalyst needed** (`brk-letter-1987`: "The market may ignore business success for a while, but eventually will confirm it") | Applies directly. Value grows from compounding at more than 25% on capital, from retained earnings, and from buybacks of about 5% of shares a year. No outside event is needed. |
| **Mr. Market serves, does not guide** (`brk-letter-1987`) | The 25% rise since mid-1988 ($37 → $46.56) is not a reason to wait or to hurry. The question is only whether today's price clears the hurdle, and it does. |
| **Work-out** (`bpl-letter-1957`) | Not applicable. There is no pending merger, liquidation or tender. |
| **Control** (`bpl-letter-1963`) | Not applicable. The market value of about $16.5B rules out control. This would be a large minority position, like the three permanent holdings (`brk-letter-1987#18`). |
| **No market forecasting** (`bpl-letter-1966-mid`, `fortune-1977-inflation`) | No view is taken on the market or the dollar. The dollar risk is handled by using 10–12% growth instead of 16–18%. |

**Practical point for Stage 5.** A position of the size discussed in Stage 1 (about $1B, roughly 6% of the company) would need to be bought gradually. The buying itself would likely push the price toward the upper value estimates. The hurdle should be re-checked against the expected average purchase price, not just today's quote.
